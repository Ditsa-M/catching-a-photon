The question

zoom 1 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Three fates for one absorbed photon

Branching ratios

Every absorbed photon ends in exactly one fate. Its yield is its rate divided by the sum of all rates.

Φᵢ = kᵢ / Σⱼ kⱼ

open PSI: Φ_CS ≈ 0.97–0.99

Today’s question

If the protein’s redox state changes one rate, where does the photon’s energy go instead?

Trapping yield of PSI: Gobets & van Grondelle, BBA 1507, 80 (2001) · CHM 598 L13, L16 · the closed-centre split is illustrative

Excited‑state kinetics

zoom 1 nm · absorb → move → separate → build → search → learn

The excited state lives for nanoseconds

Kasha’s rule

Upper states relax in femtoseconds, so emission, transfer and chemistry start from the lowest S₁: for Chl, the Qy state.

Gouterman four‑orbital model

Two HOMOs and two LUMOs mix into a strong Soret band (≈ 430 nm) and weaker Q bands (Qy ≈ 670 nm). The protein tunes Qy.

Φ_F = kᵣ / (kᵣ + k_IC + k_ISC + k_EET)

Chl a: τ ≈ 6 ns, Φ_F ≈ 0.32

→ kᵣ = Φ_F/τ ≈ 5 × 10⁷ s⁻¹

Kasha 1950 · Gouterman 1961 · Weber & Teale 1957 (Chl a Φ_F in ether)

Franck–Condon principle

zoom 1 Å · absorb → move → separate → build → search → learn

Electrons jump, nuclei wait: Franck–Condon

Vertical transitions

Electrons move far faster than nuclei. Intensity follows the overlap of vibrational wavefunctions.

I(0→v′) ∝ |⟨χ₀|χ_v′⟩|²

= e^(−S) S^v′ / v′!

S = Δ²/2, λ = S ħω

Same factor, later

This overlap becomes the Marcus term in electron transfer (lecture 16).

Franck 1926 · Condon 1926 · CHM 598 lectures 15–16 · displaced harmonic‑oscillator model, ħω = 1

Light harvesting

zoom 15 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

A lone chlorophyll absorbs ~10 photons per second

k_abs = σ · I

σ ~ 10⁻¹⁶ cm²

I ~ 10¹⁷ photons cm⁻² s⁻¹

→ k_abs ~ 10 s⁻¹ (full sun)

Nature’s fix: an antenna

Share one costly reaction centre among ~100 cheap pigments: ~100× the photon supply.

The catch

Now the energy must travel to the centre, and finish well inside 6 ns.

~10 photons s⁻¹ per Chl in full sun: Blankenship, Molecular Mechanisms of Photosynthesis (2014) · order‑of‑magnitude estimate

Excitonic coupling

zoom 1 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Excitonic coupling between neighbouring pigments

V = κ μ² / (4πε₀ n² R³)

κ = cos θ₁₂ − 3 cos θ₁ cos θ₂

E± = E₀ ± V, |±⟩ = (|eg⟩ ± |ge⟩)/√2

f± ∝ |μ₁ ± μ₂|²

J vs H aggregates

Head‑to‑tail (κ < 0): the bright state is the lower one → red shift. Side‑by‑side (κ > 0): bright state on top → blue shift.

Worked example: μ ≈ 4 D, R = 0.9 nm, κ = −2, n² ≈ 2 → V ≈ −110 cm⁻¹, enough for sub‑ps transfer. The same physics colours dye aggregates and stacked COF layers.

Kasha, Rawls & El‑Bayoumi 1965 · V(cm⁻¹) ≈ 5.04 κ μ²(D²) / n²R³(nm³), point‑dipole approximation

Energy‑transfer kinetics

zoom 1–5 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Transfer outcompetes excited‑state decay

k_F = (1/τ_D) · (R₀/R)⁶

R₀⁶ ∝ κ² Φ_D J / n⁴

E = 1 / (1 + (R/R₀)⁶)

Which regime?

|V| ≪ λ (reorganisation, disorder): incoherent Förster hops. |V| ≳ λ: delocalised excitons (Redfield). PSI’s ~1 nm Chl spacing sits in between: coupled clusters hopping to each other.

Rate competition sets the yield

Φ = 1/(1 + τ_trap/τ_loss) = 1/(1 + 25 ps / 6 ns) ≈ 0.996

Förster 1948 · regimes and PSI trapping: Gobets & van Grondelle 2001 · population curves are illustrative

Red chlorophylls

zoom 2 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Some chlorophylls sit below the trap

Low-energy “red” pigments

Strongly coupled chlorophyll clusters absorb at 708–740 nm, below P700 at 700 nm. Cyanobacterial PSI trimers have several, and the Spirulina trimer has one at ~740 nm.

k_up/k_down = (g_up/g_down) e^(−ΔE/kT)

295 K: ≈ 0.02 · 77 K: ≈ 5 × 10⁻⁷

Detailed balance

At room temperature heat lifts energy back to P700. Freeze to 77 K and it stays trapped on the red pigments.

Karapetyan, Holzwarth & Rögner, FEBS Lett. 460, 395 (1999) · Gobets & van Grondelle 2001 · CHM 598 L13 · degeneracy ratio set to 1 in the plot

11

Low-temperature fluorescence

zoom 15 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

At 77 K, red emission reports where the energy is stuck

Why freeze

At 77 K uphill transfer stops (previous slide), so red pigments keep their energy long enough to fluoresce. The F760 band becomes a direct readout.

Φ_F760 = k_F / (k_F + k_nr + k_Q)

k_Q: quenching by the reaction centre

A redox reporter

In Spirulina trimers, P700⁺ quenches F760. So the red band’s size depends on the reaction centre’s redox state.

Karapetyan et al. 1999; Shubin, Bezsmertnaya & Karapetyan, J. Photochem. Photobiol. B 30, 153 (1995) · spectra are a schematic model, not measured data

Red chlorophylls: the evidence

zoom 15 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Red pools slow trapping; cold exposes them

Same rules, three species

Five cyanobacterial PSI cores trap in 23–50 ps. Red‑Chl content alone explains the spread.

Cold strands energy

At 295 K, light up to 750 nm oxidises P700 equally well. At 5 K only about half the excitations reach P700.

Plants and models agree

Red forms in LHCI slow trapping about twofold. Models of all 96 Chls reproduce PSI’s spectra and lifetimes.

Ultrafast spectroscopy

Trapping resolved by transient absorption

Pump–probe spectroscopy

A femtosecond pump pulse starts the clock; a delayed probe pulse reads the change in absorption at each delay.

ΔA(λ,t) = Σᵢ DADSᵢ(λ) e^(−t/τᵢ)

PSI: τ_trap ≈ 20–50 ps

What it shows

The antenna signal fades as the P700⁺ band grows with the same lifetime: excitation became charge.

Reaction-centre kinetics

zoom 5 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

The first picoseconds, measured

Faster than thought

Open PSI traps in 6–9 ps, about 3× faster than assumed. Removing P_B’s Mg ligand slows only the second step, ≈2×.

A strongly coupled start

Far‑red pulses make P700⁺A₀⁻ in under 180 fs, and A₁⁻ follows in 26 ps. P700 and A₀ act as one adiabatic system.

Energetics still debated

Electrostatics put P700→A₀ and A₁A→F_X downhill. Published A₁ potentials span ≈350 mV.

Electron tunneling

zoom 1 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Tunneling through protein: Dutton’s ruler

k_ET = (2π/ħ) |H_AB|² · FC

|H_AB|² = V₀² e^(−βR), β ≈ 1.4 Å⁻¹

log₁₀k = 13 − 0.6(R − 3.6)

− 3.1 (ΔG° + λ)²/λ

Rule of thumb

0.6 decade per Å (= β/ln 10): every ~1.7 Å costs 10×. Cofactors sit ≤ 14 Å apart.

Structure matters

Ru‑labelled proteins: the fold tunes the decay (Gray & Winkler).

Moser et al., Nature 355, 796 (1992) · Moser, Page, Farid & Dutton 1995 · Page et al., Nature 402, 47 (1999) · Gray & Winkler 1996 · CHM 598 L16 · λ = 0.7 eV in plot

Charge separation

zoom 1 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Charge separation and Marcus theory

k = (2π/ħ)|H_DA|² (4πλk_BT)^(−½)

× exp[−(ΔG° + λ)² / 4λk_BT]

ΔG‡ = (ΔG° + λ)² / 4λ

Design rule

Forward steps with −ΔG° ≈ λ are activationless. Recombination with −ΔG° ≫ λ falls in the inverted region and slows down.

The cost of simulating it (Reiter 2023)

MD/QM/MM (DFT/MRCI) on all 96 Chls: energies fluctuate, pathways form transiently. Accurate, and very expensive.

Marcus 1956 · Marcus & Sutin, BBA 1985 · Reiter, Kiss, Hauer & de Vivie‑Riedle, Chem. Sci. 14, 3117 (2023)

Redox poising

zoom 3 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Solution potential sets which cofactors are reduced

Nernst on every cofactor

Each cofactor is reduced or oxidised according to how the solution potential compares with its midpoint.

f_red = 1 / (1 + e^(nF(E_h − E_m)/RT))

ferricyanide ≈ +0.36 V · dithionite ≈ −0.66 V

Open or closed

Oxidise P700 and the trap becomes a quencher. Reduce F_A/F_B and electrons cannot leave, so charge pairs recombine. Either way the photon’s energy is redistributed.

Midpoints: Brettel, BBA 1318, 322 (1997) · dithionite: Mayhew, Eur. J. Biochem. 85, 535 (1978), depends on pH and concentration · CHM 598 L13 (equilibrium)

Experimental design

zoom 1 cm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Testing redox control: titrate, freeze, measure, reverse

The protocol

Add a reductant (dithionite) in steps, measure the potential, freeze each sample to 77 K and record its emission. Then add an oxidant (ferricyanide).

Reading the result

A change that reverses with oxidant means redox control. A change that does not reverse means the protein was damaged.

Good practice

Measure E_h in the sample itself, add redox mediators so it equilibrates, and dark-adapt before freezing.

Schematic of the experimental approach, no measured data shown · Mayhew 1978 · Karapetyan et al. 1999

Kinetic model

zoom 2 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Closing the trap redistributes the photon

One state, many exits

An excitation on a red pigment can fluoresce, decay as heat, or be removed by the reaction centre. Change one exit and the others change too.

Φ₀/Φ = 1 + k_Q τ₀

τ₀ = 1/(k_F + k_nr)

What the model predicts

Making the trap less effective should brighten F760 and lengthen its lifetime. Lifetime measurements can test this separately from yield.

Stern–Volmer form of rate competition · CHM 598 L13, L16 · k_F = (15 ns)⁻¹, k_nr = (5 ns)⁻¹ are illustrative values

When the trap is closed

zoom 5 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Closed traps: quenching, safety valves, damage

P700⁺ quenches in the cold

Closing PSI raises emission 12% at room temperature but cuts it about 50% at 5 K. Below ~160 K, P700⁺ is a quencher.

Reduced acceptors are measurable

PSI’s bound Fe–S acceptors were first redox‑titrated in 1974. EPR now resolves F_X, F_A and F_B.

Protection costs efficiency

Back‑reactions trade yield for safety around O₂. PSI damage is rare in vivo but recovers very slowly.

Byrdin et al., Biophys. J. 79, 992 (2000) · Evans, Reeves & Cammack, FEBS Lett. 49, 111 (1974) · Vassiliev et al., BBA 1507, 139 (2001) · Rutherford et al., FEBS Lett. 586, 603 (2012) · Sonoike, Physiol. Plant. 142, 56 (2011)

Ongoing work

zoom 1 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Next: tuning a single red-chlorophyll site

The idea

Place a cysteine a few ångströms from one red chlorophyll, with serine as the near-identical control (O vs S).

Why cysteine

Its thiol can be oxidised or reduced. Each form carries different charge and polarity, which could shift the pigment’s site energy.

How to test

Same titrate–freeze–reverse protocol: does the F760 band shift or change size only when the thiol can change redox state?

Hypothesis and design only, no data shown · site-energy tuning by the protein environment: Gobets & van Grondelle 2001; Karapetyan et al. 2014

Design lessons from the literature

zoom 15 nm · absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Past the red limit, and wired to catalysts

Beyond 700 nm

Chl f drives PSI charge separation at 745 nm. Far‑red PSI uses light to ≈800 nm with four Chl f sites.

Wire the acceptor

Tethering F_B to a hydrogenase gives 105 e⁻ per PSI per second, twice the in‑vivo rate, by removing diffusion.

Design principles

Tune site energies, regulate flow and protect. Plant PSI–LHCI (600 kDa, 2.8 Å) shows all three in one complex.

Summary

absorb → funnel → climb → trap → poise → design

Conclusions: the trap’s redox state steers the photon

1 · Rates compete. Each fate’s yield is its share of the total rate: Φᵢ = kᵢ/Σk.

2 · Red pigments need heat. Uphill transfer follows e^(−ΔE/kT), so at 77 K they become reporters.

3 · Redox poises the trap. The Nernst equation sets which cofactors are reduced, which opens or closes the trap.

4 · Design lesson. A solar-fuel catalyst must keep its trap open: clear electrons away fast.

Each step pairs a stage of the photon’s path with the lecture concept that governs it
