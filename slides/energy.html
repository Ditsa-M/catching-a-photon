[0:40–1:50] Start with the prize. The Sun delivers about 1.2 × 10^5 terawatts to Earth; humanity used about 13.5 in 2001. Supply is not the problem; conversion is. E = hc/λ: 1239.8 divided by the wavelength in nanometres gives electronvolts, so a 680 nm photon carries 1.82 eV, 176 kJ per mole. The dashed line is 1.23 eV, the thermodynamic minimum per electron for splitting water, and you need four electrons per O2, so the readout never drops below four photons. C–H and O–H bonds are 4.3 to 4.8 eV, bigger than any visible photon. So photosynthesis works one electron at a time: eight photons move four electrons from water at +0.82 V to NADP+ at −0.32 V, storing about 4.6 of the 14.4 eV absorbed, roughly 32 percent. The rest buys speed and irreversibility, an idea that comes back at every step.
