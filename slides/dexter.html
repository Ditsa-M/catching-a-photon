[11:26–12:40] Lecture 16 contrasts two mechanisms. Förster is Coulombic: the donor relaxes and the acceptor is excited at the same time through dipole coupling, with no electrons exchanged, so it works over several nanometres. Dexter is a double electron exchange: the excited electron hops from the donor LUMO to the acceptor LUMO while an electron from the acceptor HOMO moves back to the donor. It needs wavefunction overlap, so the rate decays as e to the minus 2R over L and dies beyond about a nanometre; the plot shows the crossover. Why does it matter in biology? Spin. Triplet energy transfer, T1 plus S0 going to S0 plus T1, is forbidden by the dipole mechanism but allowed by exchange. That is why carotenoids sit in van der Waals contact with chlorophylls: they take the triplet energy by Dexter transfer before it can make singlet oxygen, then release it as heat.
