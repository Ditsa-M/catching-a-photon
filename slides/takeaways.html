[24:20–25:30] Back to the photon. It arrived with about 1.8 eV and roughly 6 ns. Light, not heat, made it an electronic isomer: a strong reductant with new selectivity. Antenna proteins funnelled it downhill, paying heat for irreversibility, in sub-picosecond hops by Förster coupling, with Dexter exchange guarding against triplets. The reaction centre trapped it in tens of picoseconds and walked the electron down a tunneling staircase, each step near −ΔG° = λ, with recombination pushed into the inverted region. When we copy that we fall short, and machine learning helps most when it encodes these same rules. Four takeaways. One: the clock rules; every step must beat decay. Two: yield is a product of steps, so screen whole systems. Three: predictions count only after synthesis and spectroscopy. Four: the frontier is ML for excited states and clean, shared data.
