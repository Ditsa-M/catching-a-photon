[26:45–27:47] Four takeaways. One: rates compete. Each fate's yield is its share of the total rate. Two: red pigments need heat to send energy uphill, following e to the minus ΔE over kT, so at 77 K they become reporters of where energy is stuck. Three: redox poises the trap. The Nernst equation sets which cofactors are reduced, and that opens or closes the reaction centre and redistributes the photon. Four: the design lesson for solar fuels is to keep the trap open by clearing electrons away fast. The animation runs through the six steps of the path we followed. If you remember one thing: the same photon, absorbed by the same pigments, can become charge, heat or light depending on whether the trap is open, and the trap's state is set by redox chemistry.
