A funnel, not a cliff

Heat buys irreversibility. P870 sits slightly uphill of LH1.

[8:01–9:24] The clearest example is the purple bacterial membrane from lecture 15. LH2 rings, solved for Rhodopseudomonas acidophila, have nine αβ pairs holding 18 B850 and 9 B800 bacteriochlorophylls plus carotenoids. They surround LH1, a larger ring absorbing at 875 nm, which encircles the reaction centre, P870. Watch an excitation: B800 to B850 in under a picosecond, LH2 to LH1 in a few picoseconds, LH1 to the reaction centre in about 35. Each step goes downhill, from 1.55 toward 1.42 eV. That is lecture 15's equation: D* plus A gives D plus A* plus heat, and the heat is the price of irreversibility. Lecture 13 gives the math: kinetics must collapse to thermodynamics, so k forward over k back equals the equilibrium constant, e to the ΔE over kT. B850 to B875 is about 42 meV, 1.6 kT, so the back rate is about a fifth of the forward rate before degeneracy. And P870 sits slightly above B875: the final trapping step is slightly uphill, paid for by thermal energy. A funnel, not a cliff. How does each hop work?
