16

Finding a material is not the same as understanding it. The last role for AI: explaining mechanism, not just ranking materials. Sumaria and colleagues (J. Phys. Chem. C 2024) studied CO2 photoreduction on CuPt nanoparticles on TiO2. The configuration space of CO2 on a bimetallic particle on an oxide is enormous, so they trained a machine-learning interatomic potential on DFT, added a bias correction, and used basin-hopping Monte Carlo to sample many adsorption geometries. The animation is a toy version of basin hopping: kick the structure, relax it downhill on the ML forces, then accept or reject the new minimum with a Metropolis test. Over a few hops it finds the deepest basin. Their result: CO2 prefers the metal-oxide interface, with carbon on a Pt site and one oxygen filling a TiO2 oxygen vacancy, and the interface stabilises *CO and CH-containing intermediates, which favours methane. They compared this with photocatalysis experiments across Cu:Pt compositions. The Behler-Parrinello idea underneath is that the total energy is a sum of atomic energies, each a learned function of that atom’s local environment. This mirrors the photosynthesis section: Reiter’s QM/MM site energies and an MLIP both buy statistical sampling that brute-force quantum chemistry can’t afford. The next frontier is ML for excited states (Westermayr and Marquetand 2021), which is exactly what pigment-protein complexes like PSI would need.
