[16:30–17:40] This is the PSI energy–kinetic diagram from lecture 16, with approximate potentials from Brettel's review. The photon lifts P700 from about +0.45 V to the excited state near −1.3 V. Then the electron walks down a staircase: to A0, a chlorophyll, in a few picoseconds; to A1, phylloquinone, in about 30; to the iron–sulfur cluster FX in 20 to 200 ns; to FA and FB in under a microsecond; and to ferredoxin in microseconds. Why not one big jump? Dutton's ruler: one long tunnel would be hopelessly slow. Short hops under 14 Å, each with driving force near λ, are fast. A0 is the redox mediator between P700* and A1 that makes this possible, and each step outruns recombination by orders of magnitude. The cost: 1.77 eV absorbed, about 0.87 eV stored between P700+ and ferredoxin. Nature spends about half the energy to buy a quantum yield near one. Can we build something that does the same?
