[3:10–4:20] How does absorption actually happen? Electrons move far faster than nuclei, so the transition is vertical: the molecule lands on the excited surface at the ground-state geometry. That is the Franck–Condon principle from lectures 15 and 16. In the animation the excited potential is displaced by Δ. The bars are the vibronic progression; each intensity is the squared overlap of vibrational wavefunctions, which for displaced harmonic oscillators is a Poisson distribution with Huang–Rhys factor S, about Δ squared over 2. Small displacement: nearly everything in the 0–0 line. Large displacement: the band broadens toward higher vibrational levels. The energy the nuclei dump as they relax, S times ħω, is a reorganisation energy, λ. Remember that symbol. In lecture 16 the same Franck–Condon factor appears in electron transfer, where at high temperature it becomes the Marcus exponential. One overlap idea controls absorption, emission, energy transfer and electron transfer.
