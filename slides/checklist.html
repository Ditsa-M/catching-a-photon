[17:40–18:40] Everything so far reduces to four steps, and an artificial photocatalyst needs the same four. Absorb: nature shares one reaction centre among a hundred pigments; a photocatalyst needs a band gap above 1.23 eV plus overpotentials, realistically 1.8 to 2.4 eV. Transfer: nature uses sub-picosecond hops; a material needs carriers to reach the surface before they recombine. Separate: nature uses a Marcus-tuned staircase; we use heterojunctions, donor–acceptor frameworks and cocatalysts. Catalyse: biology has ferredoxin, FNR and PSII's manganese cluster; we need cocatalysts that survive photocorrosion. The bars are the reality check: crops about 1 percent, the best scaled panels 0.76 percent solar to hydrogen, a 9.2 percent lab record, and the single-junction limit near 33. Different metrics, same message: one to two orders of magnitude short.
