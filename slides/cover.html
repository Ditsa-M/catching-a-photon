CHM 598 · Introduction to Photochemistry

Catching a photon before it’s lost

How photosynthesis wins a race against time, and how AI is learning the same rules to design photocatalysts

An excited molecule gets about 6 nanoseconds. This talk is about what happens inside that window.

Ditsa Mallick · [presentation date]

Open with the key number: an excited molecule has about six nanoseconds before its energy is lost as heat or light, and everything in this talk happens inside that window. Outline: first, the energetics of sunlight and the excited-state lifetime; second, how photosynthesis harvests and traps energy, with Photosystem I as the example (antenna, excitonic coupling, energy transfer, trapping measured by transient absorption, and charge separation by Marcus theory); third, the design principles this implies for artificial photocatalysts and how far current materials fall short; and fourth, how machine learning is being used to search for better photocatalysts, and why the successful studies are the ones that encode the photophysics. The tracker at the top right of each slide shows which step of the energy-conversion chain we are on.
