Light, redox and energy flow in Photosystem I

How the protein’s redox state decides where an absorbed photon’s energy goes

An excited chlorophyll has about 6 ns. An open reaction centre uses it in about 30 ps. A closed one does not.

Ditsa Mallick · [presentation date]

[0:00–1:06] An excited chlorophyll has about six nanoseconds before its energy is lost as heat or light. In Photosystem I it rarely gets that far: an open reaction centre uses the energy in about thirty picoseconds. Today's question is what happens when the reaction centre is not open. I'll argue that the redox state of the protein, which cofactors carry an extra electron, decides where an absorbed photon's energy ends up. Watch the animation: when the centre is open, excitations go to P700 and become charge; when it is closed, the energy waits, and some of it leaves as red light. The plan has six steps, shown in the tracker at the top right of every slide: absorb, funnel, climb, trap, poise and design. The first four are the photophysics from this course; the last two apply it to one question about redox control.
