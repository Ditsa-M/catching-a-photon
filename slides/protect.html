Closed traps: quenching, safety valves, damage

P700⁺ quenches in the cold

Closing PSI raises emission 12% at room temperature but cuts it about 50% at 5 K. Below ~160 K, P700⁺ is a quencher.

Reduced acceptors are measurable

PSI’s bound Fe–S acceptors were first redox‑titrated in 1974. EPR now resolves F_X, F_A and F_B.

Protection costs efficiency

Back‑reactions trade yield for safety around O₂. PSI damage is rare in vivo but recovers very slowly.

[23:36–24:16] What does a closed trap do in practice? At room temperature, oxidising P700 barely matters: the main decay goes from 34 to 37 ps. Below about 160 K it reverses and P700+ quenches, halving emission at 5 K. That is why low-temperature red emission reports on P700. The bound iron–sulfur acceptors were titrated back in 1974; they are what dithionite fills. Back-reactions act as a safety valve against oxygen chemistry, and damaged PSI recovers very slowly. A closed trap is both a probe and a risk.
