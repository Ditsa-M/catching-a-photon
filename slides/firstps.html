Faster than thought

Open PSI traps in 6–9 ps, about 3× faster than assumed. Removing P_B’s Mg ligand slows only the second step, ≈2×.

A strongly coupled start

Far‑red pulses make P700⁺A₀⁻ in under 180 fs, and A₁⁻ follows in 26 ps. P700 and A₀ act as one adiabatic system.

Energetics still debated

Electrostatics put P700→A₀ and A₁A→F_X downhill. Published A₁ potentials span ≈350 mV.

Open PSI traps in 6 to 9 ps, three times faster than assumed, and mutating P700's ligands slows only the second step, so whether the first electron leaves P700 itself is still debated. Pumping the far-red edge gives P700+A0− in under 180 fs, then A1− in 26 ps, and A1 hands over to FX in tens to hundreds of nanoseconds. Published A1 potentials span about 350 mV. The common thread is distance: 20 Å more spacing costs 10^12 in rate. So how do electrons cross protein?
