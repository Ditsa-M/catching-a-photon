Charge separation and Marcus theory

k = (2π/ħ)|H_DA|² (4πλk_BT)^(−½)

× exp[−(ΔG° + λ)² / 4λk_BT]

ΔG‡ = (ΔG° + λ)² / 4λ

Design rule

Forward steps with −ΔG° ≈ λ are activationless. Recombination with −ΔG° ≫ λ falls in the inverted region and slows down.

The cost of simulating it (Reiter 2023)

MD/QM/MM (DFT/MRCI) on all 96 Chls: energies fluctuate, pathways form transiently. Accurate, and very expensive.

[15:20–16:30] This is transition-state theory again, from lecture 13. Eyring writes k = (k_BT/h) e to the −ΔG‡/RT. Marcus supplies ΔG‡ for electron transfer: (ΔG° + λ) squared over 4λ, where λ is the reorganisation energy, the energy to distort reactants and solvent into the product geometry without moving the electron. The animation drives ΔG° more negative. The crossing point slides down to the reactant minimum, the activationless case where −ΔG° equals λ and the rate is maximal, then climbs back up: the inverted region, where more driving force makes the reaction slower. That answers lecture 16's question: the rate does not keep rising with driving force. Nature's design rule: forward steps near the top of the parabola, wasteful recombination with a huge driving force pushed into the inverted region. Reiter's MD/QM/MM simulations of all 96 PSI chlorophylls show how expensive it is even to compute the energies this needs. Let's put numbers on PSI.
